Start here

First, what is this thing?

Alacrán is an app that sits on your computer and gives your AI a memory of your business.

On its own, an AI chat window is a brilliant assistant with amnesia. Every conversation starts from nothing. Alacrán keeps the important background in one place, hands it to your AI automatically, and then lets you point that AI at the jobs you find yourself redoing every week.

Three words come up a lot in the app. Learn these and the rest is easy:

Workspace (the app calls it a “company”)
One folder on your computer holding everything about a single business: what it does, who it serves, and the jobs your AI runs for it. If you run two businesses, you make two.
Job
A task you can run over and over, like “summarise my inbox” or “write this week’s update”. You run it with a button, not by typing a prompt.
Approve
Before anything is saved, the app shows you exactly what it wants to write and waits. Nothing is ever changed behind your back.

Step 1

What you need before you start

  • A Mac running macOS 12 or later, or a Debian/Ubuntu-based Linux machine.
  • A Claude subscription. Alacrán does not sell you AI. It borrows the Claude account you already pay Anthropic for, which is why there’s no second AI bill here.
  • Claude Code, Anthropic’s free command-line tool, installed and signed in. This is the piece that actually talks to Claude for you.
  • Node.js installed. It’s a free, standard piece of software many apps rely on.
  • Optional: the gog tool, only if you want Alacrán to read your Gmail and calendar.
Don’t worry if you don’t have these yet. Alacrán checks for all of them the first time you open it and tells you exactly what’s missing, with a link for each one. You don’t need to prepare anything in advance.

Claude is the default, and the one this guide assumes. Once a workspace exists, its card has a “Runs commands with” setting where you can switch it to OpenAI’s Codex CLI or Aider instead — Aider can itself run on Anthropic, OpenAI, or a local open-source model.

Step 2

Installing it

  1. Download the .dmg file from the download button on this site.
  2. Open it. A window appears with the Alacrán icon and a shortcut to your Applications folder.
  3. Drag the icon onto Applications. That’s the install.
  4. Run one command before you open it. Open the Terminal app (press Command-Space, type “Terminal”, press Enter), paste the line below, and press Enter. Nothing visible happens — that means it worked. 
    xattr -cr "/Applications/Alacrán.app"
  5. Now open Alacrán from Applications, the same way you’d open any other app.
Why that command? Alacrán isn’t signed with a paid Apple Developer certificate, so macOS quarantines it like anything else downloaded from the internet and refuses to open it — sometimes saying the app is “damaged”. It isn’t. That line clears the quarantine flag and nothing else. Right-clicking and choosing Open is not enough for this kind of app on current macOS. You’ll need to run it once more after each update.
On Linux (Debian/Ubuntu-based), download the .deb instead and run sudo apt install ./Alacran.deb in a terminal (or sudo dpkg -i Alacran.deb && sudo apt -f install), then launch Alacrán from your applications menu.

Step 3

Opening it for the first time

Alacrán starts a small server on your own machine and opens in your normal web browser. It looks like a website, but there is no website. Everything you see is being served from your laptop, which is why it keeps working on a plane.

On first launch it runs a quick check for the things in Step 1 and shows you a short list of anything missing. Install whatever it flags, then reload the page.

Across the top you’ll see four tabs. This is the whole app:

  • Agents — your workspaces, one card each. Your home base.
  • Activity — a history of everything that has run, newest first.
  • Skills — the jobs available, and the button that runs them.
  • Connect — what’s hooked up: your AI, your Google account, GitHub.

Step 4

Setting up your first workspace

On the Agents tab, choose to add a company. Give it a name, something like Harbor Studio, and pick where on your computer it should live. Alacrán builds the folder for you, already organised, with a full change history switched on from the very first second.

That history matters more than it sounds: it’s the reason you can always undo anything the AI does, even weeks later.

Already have a folder of business notes? You can point Alacrán at an existing folder instead of making a new one, and it will adopt it rather than starting over.

Step 5

Describing your business

This is the one part that genuinely matters, and it takes about five minutes. A short guided form asks you four plain questions:

  1. What does the business actually do? One or two sentences, the way you’d explain it to a friend at a barbecue.
  2. Who is it for, and who else is involved? Your customers, your team, your suppliers.
  3. How does the work flow through? What comes in, what you do to it, what goes out the other side.
  4. What’s eating your time right now? Be honest here. This one shapes what the AI offers to take off your hands.

Every question has a real example underneath it, so you’re never staring at an empty box wondering what’s wanted. There are no wrong answers and you can rewrite any of it later.

Can’t face writing it? There’s a button that lets your AI take the first pass instead. It drafts answers based on what it already knows, shows them to you, and you correct anything it got wrong. Most people find editing a draft much easier than facing a blank page.

Step 6 · optional

Connecting your Gmail and calendar

Skip this if you only want writing help. Connect it if you want your inbox summarised.

Go to Connect. Alacrán shows you what’s linked and what isn’t, and walks you through signing in to Google on your own machine. You log in with Google directly, the normal way. Alacrán never sees your password and never stores a key.

It can only read. The Gmail connection is deliberately built read-only, so it is not capable of sending, replying, labelling, archiving or deleting. Even if you asked it to, it couldn’t. That limit is in the plumbing, not a setting someone can flip by accident.

Step 7

Running your first job

Open the Skills tab. You’ll see the jobs available to you, each with a short description. Pick one and open it. There are three tabs inside: Content shows what the job actually does, History shows previous runs, and Run is the button.

A good first one is Weekly digest if you just want to see the machinery work, or Check inbox if you connected Google in the last step.

Press Run. The button changes to Running… and a live log appears underneath so you can watch it think. Most jobs take well under a minute. You can leave the page; it keeps going.

Step 8

Reading the change before you approve it

This is the part that makes Alacrán different from most AI tools, so it’s worth understanding properly.

When the job finishes, nothing has been saved yet. Instead you get a side-by-side view of the change it wants to make: green for what it’s adding, red for anything it’s removing. You read it like you’d read a colleague’s draft.

Then you choose. Confirm & commit saves it and records a checkpoint. Cancel throws it away and nothing on your machine changes at all. If the job decided there was nothing worth changing, it says so plainly instead of inventing busywork.

You cannot break anything here. Even after you approve, every change is a checkpoint in the history, so you can go back to how things were before. There is no button in this app that permanently destroys your work in one click.

Step 9

Where your work actually lives

Everything the AI writes goes into ordinary files in the workspace folder you chose, in plain text you can open in any text editor for the next twenty years. No proprietary format, no database only Alacrán can read.

That means you can open the folder in Finder any time, read it, edit it by hand, back it up to Dropbox, or walk away from Alacrán entirely and still have everything. Your work is not locked inside this app.

Step 10

The jobs you get out of the box

  • Check inbox — a plain summary of what’s unread and what looks like it needs you. Read-only.
  • Weekly digest — turns a week of scattered activity into the update you were going to write on Friday.
  • Record a decision — captures a decision and, more importantly, why you made it, so next quarter you remember the reasoning.
  • Retrospective — a structured look back at what worked and what didn’t, kept as a record rather than lost in a doc.
  • Session handoff — writes down where you got to, so tomorrow’s you (or your AI) picks up without re-reading everything.
  • Define company — the one from Step 5. Re-run it any time the business changes shape.

Step 11

Running more than one business

Add as many workspaces as you like. A consultancy and a side project. Three clients. Each keeps its own context, its own connected tools and its own history, and they never see each other.

That separation is the point: a client’s details can’t wander into someone else’s weekly report, because as far as each workspace is concerned, the others don’t exist.

Step 12

Backing up and moving to a new computer

Because everything lives on your machine, backing up is on you, and Alacrán makes it one click.

  1. Connect a GitHub account on the Connect tab. Free accounts are fine.
  2. Hit back up on a workspace. It creates a private repository, visible only to you, and pushes everything into it.
  3. On the new computer, install Alacrán, connect the same GitHub account, and restore. Your whole workspace comes back, history and all.
Private means private: the repository is created locked-down by default, and nobody, including us, can see inside it.

Step 13

Going further: teaching it your own routines

Once the built-in jobs feel natural, the real payoff is teaching it yours. Anything you do repeatedly in roughly the same shape is a candidate.

The pattern is always the same. Do the task with your AI once, in conversation, until the result is genuinely good. Then save that way of working as a reusable job, so next time it’s a button instead of a conversation. You can also describe what you want and let Alacrán draft the first version for you.

People commonly end up with things like:

  • A monthly newsletter drafted in their own voice, because the AI has read the business.
  • A follow-up email that actually references the last conversation with that client.
  • A standing Monday briefing pulling together the inbox, the calendar and last week’s notes.
  • Campaign briefs and social captions written to the same format every single time.

A saved job can be dropped onto another workspace in one click, which is how a routine you worked out for one client becomes standard practice across all of them.

Keeping it current

Updating to a new version

Alacrán tells you when a new version is out: a small strip appears at the top of the app with a download link. It can’t install the update for you yet, so the swap itself is manual, and it takes about twenty seconds:

  1. Download the latest .dmg from the site. That link always points at the newest release.
  2. Quit Alacrán if it’s running.
  3. Open the .dmg and drag the icon onto Applications, exactly as you did the first time. macOS asks whether to replace the existing app. Say yes.
  4. Run the same Terminal command as the first install — the new copy is a fresh download, so it’s quarantined too: 
    xattr -cr "/Applications/Alacrán.app"
  5. Open it again. That’s the whole update.
Your work is not affected by this. Your workspaces live in folders you chose, and your settings live outside the app bundle, so replacing the program never touches either. You don’t need to export anything first, and you won’t be asked to set anything up again.

To check which version you’re on, find Alacrán in Applications, press Command-I, and read the version at the top of the info window.

Don’t want to be told? Dismiss the strip and it won’t mention that version again, though it will still speak up for a later one. The check itself is an anonymous read of a public page, at most once a day, and it sends nothing about you.
On Linux, download the latest .deb from the site and run sudo apt install ./Alacran.deb again — it updates in place, the same command as the first install.

Step 14

When something goes wrong

  • It says something is missing on first launch. Install whatever it names and reload. That screen is a checklist, not an error.
  • A job produced something wrong. Press Cancel. Nothing was saved. Nothing to undo.
  • You approved something you regret. Every approval is a checkpoint, so it can be rolled back. Nothing is truly gone.
  • The inbox summary is empty. Check the Google connection on the Connect tab; the sign-in may have expired.
  • Still stuck? Email contact@alacran.ai and a human will answer.

Set it up once this afternoon. Every week after that, the routine work is already done by the time you sit down.
