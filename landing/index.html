An AI-native framework that stays on your machine

Your AI forgets.
Alacrán remembers.

Alacrán is an AI-native framework for organizing and running your AI company — entirely on your own machine. Every file it touches lives in a folder you own: nothing uploaded, nothing kept anywhere else. It hands your AI the jobs you repeat every week, the inbox summary, the weekly update, the client follow-up, and nothing gets saved until you approve it.

In plain English

Never used an AI tool before? Start here.

You already know how to ask ChatGPT or Claude a question. The catch is that it starts every conversation as a total stranger. It doesn’t know what you sell, who your customers are, or how you like things written, so you end up typing the same background paragraph over and over before it can be any use.

Alacrán is a small app for your Mac that fixes exactly that. You write down the basics of your business once, in ordinary words. From then on your AI reads that first, every single time, and gets straight to the actual work.

Without it

  • You re-explain the business in every new chat
  • You copy answers out of a chat window by hand
  • Last month’s good draft is lost in old conversations
  • The tone comes out slightly wrong every time

With it

  • It already knows the business before you ask
  • The work is saved where you can find it later
  • Anything it did well becomes a one-click repeat
  • You read the change and approve it, or you don’t

No coding, at any point. If you can fill in a form and read a paragraph before clicking Yes, you can run this. It all happens on your own laptop, and we don’t run a server your business could reach, so there is nowhere for your data to go.

Privacy, specifically

We couldn’t spy on you if we wanted to.

Most apps say “we take privacy seriously”. Here is the actual architecture instead. Alacrán runs as a small server on your own Mac, reachable only from your machine. We do not operate a server your business data could reach, so there is no database of ours with your work in it, and no admin panel anyone here could open.

There is no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting anywhere in the app. Not disabled by default. Not present at all.

So we won’t claim “nothing ever leaves your Mac”, because you’d be right to be sceptical. Here is the honest, complete list of what does:

And you don’t have to take our word for any of it. The whole source is public — go read it.

A version number check, to GitHub, at most once a dayYour prompts, to whichever AI that workspace is set to — Anthropic by default — when you press RunYour files, to your own private GitHub, when you press Back up

That’s the whole list. Everything else stays in a folder you picked, in plain text you can read without us.

Questions

The things people actually ask.

Straight answers, including the awkward ones.

I’ve never used an AI tool. Is this going to be over my head?

No. If you can fill in a form and read a paragraph before clicking Yes, you can use Alacrán. There is no code, no prompt writing, and no jargon to learn.

Setup is four plain questions about your business, in ordinary words. After that, running a job is pressing a button and reading what comes back. Our complete guide walks through every screen assuming you’ve never touched an AI tool in your life.

Can you see my business data?

No, and not because we promise not to. We have no way to.

Alacrán runs on your Mac, listening only to your own machine. We don’t operate a server your company data could be sent to, which means there is no database on our side holding your work and no internal tool any employee of ours could use to look at it. The data sits in a folder you chose, on your laptop.

Alright, so what does leave my Mac?

Fair question, and here’s the complete list rather than a vague reassurance:

1. A version number is checked against GitHub’s public releases page, at most once a day, so the app can tell you when an update is out. It’s an anonymous read of a public page. Nothing is sent about you, and you can turn it off.

2. Your prompts go to whichever AI that workspace is set to when you press Run — Anthropic by default, or OpenAI / wherever Aider is pointed if you’ve switched it. Either way, that’s the AI doing its job, exactly as if you’d typed into it yourself. Nothing is sent when you’re just clicking around the app.

3. Your files go to GitHub only if you press Back up, and only into a private repository on your own account.

That is genuinely everything. There is no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting in the app at all — and since the source is public, that’s a claim you can check rather than trust.

Do I have to pay for AI on top of this?

You need an account with whichever AI a workspace uses. By default that’s your own Claude subscription from Anthropic, which many people already have, and it’s the option we’ve run the longest.

We deliberately don’t resell AI usage, so there is no bill from us at all — the app itself is free and open source. You pay your AI provider for the AI, and nothing to us, ever.

Can I use ChatGPT, or my own open-source model, instead of Claude?

Yes. Every workspace has a “Runs commands with” setting on its card: Claude Code by default, or OpenAI’s Codex CLI, or Aider. Aider itself can run on Anthropic, OpenAI, or a local model you host yourself, so it’s the one to reach for if you want to keep everything off the big providers entirely.

Whichever you pick, the deal stays the same as Claude: your own account, your own key, nothing routed through us. Claude Code is the one we’ve run longest and trust most by default; the others are newer here, so if something looks off, email us and tell us what happened.

What if the AI gets something wrong?

Then you don’t approve it, and nothing happens. This is the core design of the app rather than a safety feature bolted on.

When a job finishes, nothing has been saved yet. You see exactly what it wants to change, laid out clearly, and you either accept it or throw it away. If you accept something and regret it later, every approval is saved as a checkpoint you can roll back to.

Could it email a client or delete something by accident?

It can’t email anyone. The Gmail connection is built read-only, so sending, replying, labelling, archiving and deleting are not capabilities it has. That isn’t a checkbox someone could tick by mistake; the ability simply isn’t there.

For files, nothing is written until you approve it, and everything that is written becomes a checkpoint you can undo.

Does it work without internet?

Partly, and here’s the honest split. The app itself, your files and your whole history are local, so you can open it and read everything on a plane.

Anything that asks the AI to do something needs a connection, because that request goes to Anthropic. Nothing else does — there’s no licence check, no account, and no sign-in, so the app never locks you out for being offline.

How do I get updates?

Today, you update it yourself, and it takes about twenty seconds. Download the latest version from this site, drag it into Applications the same way you did the first time, say yes to replacing the old one, then re-run the same xattr -cr command you ran on the first install — the new copy is a fresh download, so macOS quarantines it too.

Your work is completely untouched by this. Your workspaces live in your own folders and your settings live outside the app, so replacing the app is genuinely just swapping the program. Nothing to export, nothing to migrate.

The app now tells you when a new version is out: a small strip appears at the top with a download link, and you can dismiss it. It won’t pester you about the same version twice, and the check is an anonymous read of a public page, at most once a day.

What happens when I get a new laptop?

Press Back up before you switch. That creates a private repository on your own GitHub account and pushes your workspace into it.

On the new Mac, install Alacrán, sign in to the same GitHub account, and restore. Everything comes back, including the full history. Switching machines used to mean starting over, which is exactly why we built this.

Do I need to know what “git” is?

Not at all. Git is the technology underneath, the same thing software teams use to track changes, and it’s what gives you the undo button and the full history.

You never have to touch it or learn a single command. You’ll only ever see it as “approve this change” and “here’s what happened last week”.

If I stop paying, do I lose my work?

No. Everything the app produces is ordinary text files sitting in a folder you chose on your own Mac. You can open them in any text editor, today or in ten years.

There’s no proprietary format and no export process to go through, because your work was never locked inside the app to begin with.

Is there a Windows or Linux version?

Linux, yes — there’s a .deb for Debian/Ubuntu-based distros, right alongside the Mac download above. Windows isn’t built yet. If you’d like it, email us, because that’s genuinely how we’re deciding what to build next.

macOS says Alacrán is damaged, or won’t open it. What now?

It isn’t damaged. Alacrán isn’t signed with a paid Apple Developer certificate, so macOS quarantines it the way it does anything downloaded from the internet, and then refuses to open it. Drag the app into Applications, then open Terminal (Command-Space, type “Terminal”, Enter), paste this line and press Enter:

xattr -cr "/Applications/Alacrán.app"

Nothing visible happens, which means it worked. Now open the app normally. You’ll need to run it once more after each update, since every new download gets quarantined again.

That command clears the quarantine flag and does nothing else. Right-clicking and choosing Open, which works for some unsigned apps, is not enough for this one on current macOS. The real fix is notarization, which needs a paid Apple developer account — it’s an open item, not a permanent state of affairs.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Alacrán is free and open source under the MIT licence. There is no account, no licence key, no trial clock and no paid tier — download it and it works.

You do still pay whoever provides your AI (an Anthropic or OpenAI subscription, or nothing at all if you point it at a local model). That bill is between you and them; we never see it.

It’s open source — can I change it?

Yes. The MIT licence lets you use, modify, fork and even sell it. The source lives on GitHub, along with the design specs for every feature that shipped, so you can see not just what the code does but why it’s shaped that way.

Bug reports and pull requests are genuinely welcome — see CONTRIBUTING.md in the repository.
