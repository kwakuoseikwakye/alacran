Intro
What is this? — a 30-second explanation
⏱ about 3 min ▼

An AI company is a small company-shaped setup where you have an AI (artificial intelligence) work like a "staff member."

  1. You (the boss) give the AI instructions: "do this."
  2. The AI follows the instructions: it drafts documents, does calculations, keeps records.
  3. Only the important decisions (spending money, signing a contract) always get a final OK from you.

This repository (= a bundle of folders) is the template for that company setup. Think of it like a blank submission form for a manga magazine. Only the frame is provided, and the content (your company's information) is something we're about to fill in together.

Only 3 characters to remember

CharacterRoleThink of it like
YouThe boss. Gives instructions, gives the final OKA teacher assigning classroom duties
Claude CodeThe AI employee. Receives instructions and actually does the workA diligent, hard-working new hire
GitHubThe record-keeper. Stores every to-do list and every document createdA classroom blackboard + a locker
You (the boss) │ "Do this" ▼ Claude Code (the AI employee) │ "Done!" ▼ GitHub (the record-keeper's blackboard) │ Every record stays here

Why call it a "company"?

"It's a hassle explaining the same thing to the AI every time." "I forget how far we got yesterday." "The AI does too much on its own and that causes trouble." — all of these happen because there's no "company structure" in place.

🏢 A company-like structure = a structure where the AI doesn't get lost

A company has in-house rules, so a new hire doesn't get lost. It has a handover notebook, so work continues even when the person in charge changes. It has a manager's approval, so money doesn't get spent without oversight. This template is a box that prepares these for an AI.

Learn the structure
The flow of work (5 steps) and 6 promises
⏱ about 5 min ▼

Whatever the job is, it just cycles through these 5 phases.

① Define → ② Plan → ③ Execute → ④ Verify → ⑤ Record │ ┌──────────────────────┘ ▼ on to the next cycle
PhaseWhat you doThink of it like
① DefineWrite out "here's what kind of company we are"Making a business card
② PlanWrite "we're going to do this next" onto GitHub (making a work ticket = an Issue)Writing a shopping list
③ ExecuteActually do the work, together with Claude CodeGoing shopping
④ VerifyHave a machine check "did it actually get done right?"Checking the amount against the receipt
⑤ RecordWrite down "here's how far I got today"Writing it into a household ledger

Small jobs (fixing a single typo, say) can skip ① and ②. But for a big job, or one you can't undo afterward, always go through starting from ①, in order.

6 important promises

You don't have to memorize all of them — at first it's fine to just know their names.

PromiseIn one line
Issue-FirstWork always starts by making a ticket on GitHub first
HITL GateMoney, contracts, and irreversible operations always get a human's OK
SSOTImportant information is kept together in "just one place"
Scope ContractDeclare "how far you're going" before you start
No fake greenNever let it pretend to work when it isn't actually working
Session handoverWrite a letter to your future self at the end of a work session
⚠️ HITL Gate (human approval) is especially important

The AI is fast, but before spending money or deleting important data, it's always set up to stop and ask you first.

Learn the structure
The memo box (notes/) — a place to toss in whatever crosses your mind
⏱ about 5 min ▼

Separate from the company's proper "document shelf" definitions/ (a staff roster and product catalog), there's a "memo-pad shelf" too. That's notes/. It's a place for right after a meeting, or on the move — for when you suddenly think "oh, I should jot that down."

The only place you're free to write in is the "memo box"

  1. Remember: the only place you (the boss) are free to write into is notes/inbox/ (the memo box).
  2. Don't worry about format — a quick scribble is fine. No need to feel pressure to "write it neatly."
  3. You don't write directly into the other small rooms (the company's story, other companies' info, customer notes, procedure documents) — those are places the AI employee tidies up for you.

You can also write from your phone or tablet

Opening this folder with a free note-taking app called Obsidian lets you drop in a memo even while you're out. You don't need Obsidian for this — it's also fine to just paste it in from your phone's built-in notes app when you're back at your computer. The detailed syncing steps are written in notes/inbox/README.md.

The AI employee tidies up whatever you toss in

The next time you start working with Claude Code, this magic word has it check the memo box's contents:

/ingest-context inbox

The AI employee checks whether the memo has anything confidential mixed in (real names, amounts, passwords, etc.), and then tidies it into the correct small room.

The memo's contentWhere it gets tidied to
Your own company's story, strategy memosThe "company story" shelf (notes/company/)
Information about other companies, competitors, the marketThe "other-company info" shelf (notes/market/)
Notes from a meeting with a customerThe "customer notes" shelf (notes/clients/)
Work proceduresThe "procedure document" shelf (notes/sops/)
💡 Designed so it's fine to leave things sitting

If a memo sits in the memo box for 7 days or more, /verify (the check machine) tells you "things are piling up." In other words, you only need to "toss it in for now" — tidying up can be left to the AI employee.

🚫 An important warning — things you must not write even in the memo box

The memo box gets saved onto git (the record-keeper's blackboard). Never write real names, amounts, or passwords, even in the memo box. Credentials go in a separate "safe" called secrets/ instead (that never lands on the blackboard). When in doubt, remember "it goes in secrets/," and you'll be safe.

Hands-on (Steps 1-4)
Gather your tools, and start up
⏱ about 20 min ▼

Step 1. Gather your tools (a shopping list)

ToolWhat it doesHow to get it
GitA tool that keeps a history of your filesDownload from git-scm.com
Python 3The language that runs the check scriptsDownload from python.org
Claude CodeThe AI employee itselfSign up for a Claude monthly plan, and download from the official site
A GitHub accountThe record-keeper's blackboardSign up for free at github.com
GitHub CLI (gh)A tool to operate GitHub from your computerDownload from cli.github.com

How to check — open the "Terminal" (the black screen) on your computer, and type this:

git --version && python3 --version && claude --version && gh --version

If each one shows a version number, that tool is installed. If it says "command not found," it isn't installed yet.

Step 2. Create your own repository

  1. On the GitHub screen, press this template's "Use this template" button.
  2. Be sure to choose "Private" (since this will handle your company's information, it's safer to keep it out of public view). Name it whatever you like (e.g. my-ai-company).
  3. Copy it (clone it) onto your own computer:
git clone git@github.com:<your-account-name>/<repository-name>.git && cd <repository-name>

Step 3. Start up Claude Code

claude

Claude Code starts up and greets you. At this point it automatically reads CLAUDE.md (the company's constitution). By the time it greets you, your company's rules are already in its head.

Step 4. Try running the check machine, just once for now

/verify
💡 Full of "INFO" at first — that's correct

This is the magic word that has a machine check "is our company actually following the rules?" At first, almost everything shows "INFO (skipped, since there's no data yet)." That's the correct result. As you fill in more content going forward, "PASS" gradually increases.

5
Hands-on (Steps 5-9)
Define your company, and run your first cycle
⏱ about 30 min ▼

Step 5. Define your own company (the most important step!)

/define-company

Claude Code asks you 4 questions, one at a time:

  1. What problem does your company solve?
    e.g. "We make tools that make recipe management and inventory management easier for the neighborhood bakery."
  2. Who are your main customers?
    e.g. "The owner of a small neighborhood bakery."
  3. What flow do you create value through?
    e.g. "Enter a recipe -> check ingredient stock -> get an order alert."
  4. What's the biggest thing you're stuck on right now?
    e.g. "Ordering is done by gut feeling, so we end up with too much or too little of an ingredient."

No need to rush. Based on your answers, it creates a "company business-card file" called definitions/ontology/company.yaml.

Step 6. Look over the finished business card

Open definitions/ontology/company.yaml, and look it over — "does this seem right?" If anything's off, it's fine to rewrite it right there. At this point, the AI employee now knows about your company.

Step 7. Make your first work ticket (an Issue)

Let's try doing some small piece of work. Following the Issue-First principle, make a ticket first:

Create a new Issue: "Add a self-introduction to HANDOFF.md"

Claude Code files an Issue on GitHub for you. Now you have a reservation for the work.

Step 8. Do the work

Go ahead and work on the Issue you just created

Before starting, Claude Code always declares "I'm going to change this, and I'm not going to touch that" (= the Scope Contract promise). You look it over, and if it's OK, just answer "go ahead." Once done, it automatically commits (= saves) it.

Step 9. Leave a record and finish up

/handoff

HANDOFF.md (the handover notebook) gets updated, leaving a note saying "here's how far I got today. Start from here next." Next session, you can just start by reading this notebook.

🎉 Great work!

That's your first cycle, all the way around!

6
Get comfortable
Things worth trying by day 3
⏱ 3 days ▼

To get comfortable in the first 1-3 days, we recommend trying things in this order.

DayWhat to doMagic words used
Day 1Go through "Steps 1-9" all the way around once/define-company /verify /handoff
Day 2Try breaking a bigger piece of work down into an Epic (a parent ticket)/create-epic
Day 3Practice recording an important decision. Practice filing a memo directly onto a proper shelf, in a well-formed way, while the AI asks you questions/decision /retro /stock-note
Once comfortableHave the AI put together a summary (like a weekly report) of the memos and decisions that piled up this week/digest

This lets you experience all 5 phases once each. Once you've been through them, you'll pick up the rhythm of "ah, so this is how it cycles."

Troubleshooting
Escape routes for when you get stuck
▼
SymptomFix
It feels like Claude Code isn't following the rules Try asking "Did you read CLAUDE.md? Tell me the 5 phases and 6 principles." If it can answer, it knows the rules. If it can't, have it Read CLAUDE.md
/verify said "FAIL" Always read what it says. "It's a hassle, let's just delete it" is not OK (= a violation of the no-fake-green promise). Paste the FAIL's content and ask "how do I fix this?" — it will tell you how
Claude Code is trying to handle money or a contract on its own Stop it. That's an operation that needs the HITL Gate (human approval). Say "I'll decide this — stop," and it will stop
You're not sure what to write where CLAUDE.md's §4.5, "the context map," has a table mapping "where does this information go?"

This template doesn't yet have the "right answer" filled in.

You're the one who creates the right answer. And Claude Code (the AI employee) is what helps you organize it, together.

The tools are all here. All that's left is to put your company in.
